## M1.1 Standard and compound units

Every measurement question is really two questions: what quantity is this, and what unit is it sensible to measure it in? Pick a unit that keeps the number a manageable size.

| Quantity | Common units |
|---|---|
| Mass | mg, g, kg, t |
| Force | N |
| Length | mm, cm, m, km |
| Area | mm$^2$, cm$^2$, m$^2$, km$^2$ |
| Capacity | ml, l |
| Volume | mm$^3$, cm$^3$, m$^3$ (also ml, l) |
| Time | seconds, minutes, hours, days, weeks, months, years |

<Tip title="Match the unit to the scale">
A swimming pool's volume is measured in m$^3$, a kitchen floor's area in m$^2$, and a can of drink's volume in ml — using cm$^3$ or mm$^2$ for these would just produce unwieldy numbers.
</Tip>

For capacity and volume specifically:

$$
1 \text{ ml} = 1 \text{ cm}^3, \qquad 1 \text{ l} = 1000 \text{ cm}^3, \qquad 1000 \text{ l} = 1 \text{ m}^3
$$

### Compound units

A compound unit combines two measurements, such as metres and seconds into metres per second. It is always read as "per": km h$^{-1}$ means kilometres *per* hour.

<FormulaCard title="Common compound units">
$$
\text{density} = \frac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}, \qquad
\text{average speed} = \frac{\text{distance travelled}}{\text{time taken}}
$$

$$
\text{rate of pay} = \frac{\text{pay received}}{\text{time worked}}, \qquad
\text{pressure} = \frac{\text{perpendicular force}}{\text{area}}
$$
</FormulaCard>

<WorkedExample title="Unit cost of an item">
50 boxes of sweets cost £215. What is the unit cost of a box?

The unit cost is the cost of 1 box, so divide the total cost by the number of items:

$$
\frac{215}{50} = 4.30
$$

A box costs £4.30.
</WorkedExample>

<Shortcut title="Reading off the units tells you the operation">
If mass is in grams and volume is in cm$^3$, density comes out in g cm$^{-3}$ automatically — you don't need to remember the formula direction, just divide the first quantity by the second.
</Shortcut>

## M1.2 Converting between units

### Standard unit conversions

| Type | Conversions |
|---|---|
| Length | 1 km = 1000 m; 1 m = 100 cm = 1000 mm; 1 cm = 10 mm |
| Area | 1 km$^2$ = 1 000 000 m$^2$; 1 m$^2$ = 10 000 cm$^2$; 1 cm$^2$ = 100 mm$^2$ |
| Volume/capacity | 1 l = 1000 ml = 1000 cm$^3$; 1000 l = 1 m$^3$; 1 m$^3$ = 1 000 000 cm$^3$ |
| Mass | 1 kg = 1000 g; 1 g = 1000 mg |
| Time | 60 s = 1 min; 60 min = 1 hour; 24 hours = 1 day; 7 days = 1 week |

<Trap title="Area and volume conversions are not the length factor">
Converting length multiplies by the linear factor once, but area squares it and volume cubes it. Since $1 \text{ m} = 100 \text{ cm}$, it follows that $1 \text{ m}^2 = 100^2 \text{ cm}^2 = 10\,000 \text{ cm}^2$ and $1 \text{ m}^3 = 100^3 \text{ cm}^3 = 1\,000\,000 \text{ cm}^3$.
</Trap>

### Compound unit conversions

To convert a compound unit, convert each part separately, then recombine.

<WorkedExample title="Density in different units">
A piece of metal has density $20 \text{ g cm}^{-3}$. What is this in kg m$^{-3}$?

Write it as a fraction and convert each unit:

$$
20 \text{ g cm}^{-3} = \frac{20 \text{ g}}{1 \text{ cm}^3} = \frac{20 \div 1000 \text{ kg}}{1 \div 1\,000\,000 \text{ m}^3} = \frac{0.02 \text{ kg}}{0.000001 \text{ m}^3} = 20\,000 \text{ kg m}^{-3}
$$
</WorkedExample>

<WorkedExample title="Average speed with mixed units">
A car travels 35 000 m in 30 minutes. What is its average speed in km h$^{-1}$?

Convert distance to km and time to hours first:

$$
35\,000 \text{ m} = 35 \text{ km}, \qquad 30 \text{ min} = 0.5 \text{ hours}
$$

Then apply the formula:

$$
\text{average speed} = \frac{35}{0.5} = 70 \text{ km h}^{-1}
$$
</WorkedExample>

## Formula and shortcut summary

- $\text{density} = \dfrac{\text{mass}}{\text{volume}}$
- $\text{average speed} = \dfrac{\text{distance}}{\text{time}}$
- $\text{rate of pay} = \dfrac{\text{pay}}{\text{time worked}}$
- $\text{pressure} = \dfrac{\text{force}}{\text{area}}$
- $\text{unit cost} = \dfrac{\text{total cost}}{\text{number of items}}$
- Length $\times 1$, area $\times$ (factor)$^2$, volume $\times$ (factor)$^3$ when converting
